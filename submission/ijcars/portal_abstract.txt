Purpose: Repeated recordings from the same operator can permit surgical-performance models to use operator-specific motion patterns. We tested whether operator identity was recoverable from surgical kinematics and whether suppressing that information improved prediction for unseen operators. Methods: We retrospectively analyzed 2,501 dry-lab trials representing 92 datasetparticipants from JIGSAWS, ROSMA, BGU-PatCut, and BGU-SKILLS. Outcomes were expertrated Global Rating Scale, observed task errors, and image-derived cutting error; BGU-SKILLS contributed to identity analysis only. We compared same-participant with held-out-participant evaluation, used fresh linear and random-forest identity probes and JIGSAWS label permutations, and evaluated duration removal, duration residualization, linear identity-subspace projection (I3), and adversarial suppression (I4). Intervals used 2,000 participant bootstrap replicates. Results: Unsuppressed identity accuracy ranged from 0.228–0.944 for linear probes and 0.381–0.844 for nonlinear probes across ten dataset-task groups. JIGSAWS participant-wise permutations retained substantially more same-participant skill accuracy than record-wise permutations (median differences 0.444–0.538). I3 reduced fresh linear identity accuracy by 0.184 (95% interval 0.153–0.214) and nonlinear accuracy by 0.138 (0.100–0.175); I4 failed the prespecified two-probe rule. Held-out-participant standardized mean absolute error favored I3 in one of seven tasks and the unsuppressed model in six; six intervals crossed zero. Conclusion: Identity was recoverable and targeted projection reduced tested identity recovery, but suppression did not consistently improve unseen-operator prediction. Identity diagnostics and held-out-user utility should be evaluated separately.
